# The Life Simulator — the Military Depth update

*Everything since the last itch.io build (v186.1). For the devlog / update notes.*

---

This one took the military side of the game from "you enlist and things happen to you"
to something with a unit, a record, and people in it who have their own lives.

Your existing saves will keep working. New games will play out differently from before —
that's expected, the simulation itself changed underneath.

---

## Your unit is real now

You get posted to an actual unit with an actual roster, and it stays with you. The men
beside you are registered people with families, homes, careers before they enlisted, and
records of their own.

When you deploy, **they deploy with you.** That sounds obvious, but it wasn't true before:
your squad existed only on your record, so if you clicked a squadmate's profile after a
tour, he had never been to the war you just fought together. Now he has his own tour, his
own history, and his own version of what happened.

They also get hit. And when one of them is hurt badly enough, he's evacuated and he is
*gone* — off the roster, sent home, not standing on the line the next month like nothing
happened.

## Combat is written, not rolled

A contact used to be a threat level and a result. Now it's a situation: where you are,
what's happening, and what you can do about it. You pick, and then you find out.

Three rules I set for the writing after reading my own screen and wincing:

- You are never told the outcome before you choose
- The same choice doesn't produce the same sentence every time
- The grammar actually makes sense

Wounds are written in detail too — what kind, where, and what it leaves behind. Recovery
is real; so is the thing that doesn't fully heal.

Every action now files an **after-action report** you can go back and read.

## Peacetime is a career, not a waiting room

Most of a service career isn't war, so most of a service career now has something in it:

- Garrison duty, inspections, and a unit grade your unit can genuinely fail
- Promotion boards
- Command — when people are assigned to you, they're specific named people
- Special duty, including a recruiting tour where you sign up the town's kids
- Service schools and courses that can wash you out or recycle you
- ROTC, offered rather than imposed

## Unit awards

Your unit earns decorations as a unit, and they're worn on the unit's rack — not quietly
mixed in with the ones you earned yourself. Two different things, kept in two different
places.

## The wider world

Forty nations now, and their alignments drift over time rather than sitting frozen from
the day the world was made. Wars leave something behind when they end.

## Money adds up

The monthly statement is now a real statement — the numbers on it match what actually
moved through your accounts, to the cent. Several silent debits that never appeared
anywhere have been closed.

## Mobile

A lot of the deeper screens had become long columns you had to scroll past to reach
anything. Records, schools, unit awards and the rest now collapse into tappable sections
that open when you want them. Much less thumb-work on a phone.

---

## Fixes

- **Every new game was handed the same world.** Starting a fresh game used a fixed seed
  unless you dug into advanced settings, so every player's history played out identically.
  New games now get a genuinely random world.
- **Civilian accidents counted as service injuries.** Falling off a ladder as a veteran
  built a service disability rating and paid a pension for it. Provenance now follows how
  you were actually hurt.
- **A mugging could give you shrapnel wounds.** Violent crime was borrowing the combat
  injury table. It has its own now.
- **Someone in your own household was buying up your company.** The yearly "a rival comes
  for your business" event could pick your own spouse, spending your own savings — which
  also blocked any real rival from ever coming for it.
- **You took 8 of every 10 casualties.** Fixed; the squad takes its share.
- **Frostbite was treated as life-threatening.** It is not.
- **You could die of a broken foot.** You cannot.
- Squadmates listed as wounded now have it on their own record, not just yours.
- Assorted wording and placement fixes on the service record.
