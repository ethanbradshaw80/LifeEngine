# The Life Simulator — the Work & Money update

*Everything since the last itch.io build (v177). For the devlog / update notes.*

---

## Seventy-four careers, and a ladder under every one

Work used to be a job title and a wage. Now it is a trade you enter at the
bottom and climb.

- **74 career ladders, 310 rungs**, across fifteen fields — technology,
  trades, healthcare, hospitality, finance, the law, teaching, the creative
  work, public service, farming, and the rest. A cashier can end up running a
  region. A plumber's helper can end up a contractor with his own vans.
- **Every rung tells you what it wants**: the months you need behind you, the
  skills, the schooling, the papers. Nothing is hidden — you can look at the
  top of a ladder on your first day and see exactly what stands between you
  and it.
- **Eighteen skills, earned only by doing the work.** Leadership, negotiation,
  attention to detail, medical knowledge, and so on. They cannot be bought and
  they cannot be rushed: mastery takes a career, and the work you do decides
  what you get good at.
- **Thirteen licences you can go and sit for** — a commercial driving licence,
  cosmetology, a pilot's licence, a gaming licence, a firefighter's
  certification. Money and months, and no amount of talent substitutes.
- **Changing trade starts you at the bottom of the new one.** What twenty
  years buys you is speed, not a higher seat: you already clear the skill
  gates above, so only the months hold you back.

## You get interviewed now

- Applying opens an interview, and **the room can say no**. Work is not handed
  over because your schooling happens to qualify you.
- **The place that hires you fits the trade.** A nurse is taken on at the
  county hospital, a machinist at the machine shop. No more being hired as a
  cashier by the courthouse.

## And the whole town climbs the same ladders

Townspeople take the same paths, are taught by the same work, and are promoted
by the same gates you are. They sit their own licences out of their own
pockets. A promotion rule that binds only the player is a penalty wearing a
rule's clothes.

## Your money, itemised and honest

The money screens have been rebuilt around one idea: **every number should say
where it came from.**

- **"The month" is a real statement now.** Every pound that moved, with a
  label — your pay after tax, what the business drew, rent from your tenants,
  interest on your savings, the shop, the school fees, the trust. Not a
  forecast of a typical month: what actually happened.
- **It is YOUR money, not the household's.** A grown man living at home was
  being shown his parents' eleven mouths and their whole bill. Now the card
  counts you, your partner and the children who actually depend on you.
- **School fees have their own line.** They can be half of what looked like
  "living costs", and a line you cannot check is a line you cannot trust.
- **Tax is counted once**, on the wage it came off.
- **Net worth is itemised** — your cash, investments, retirement, every
  property you hold, and your share of any business — and if you share a
  purse with your spouse, the screen says so and shows the joint total.

## Something to do with the money

Three new ways to spend a fortune, on the new **Legacy** tab.

- **Give to the town.** The school, the courthouse, the library — a gift, a
  new wing, or endow one **in your family's name for good**. The name goes on
  once and it outlives you.
- **Build your own house.** At the top of the market you stop shopping and
  start building: a house, an estate, or a **manor** — nothing like which has
  ever stood in this county. You choose the street and pay the builder's
  premium.
- **Settle a family trust.** An estate is settled once — taxed, split between
  your children, gone. A trust never passes through anybody's estate. It pays
  your line for as long as there is one, down to grandchildren who were not
  born when you wrote it. The money is not yours any more: you cannot draw on
  it and it is not yours to leave. That is the whole trade.

## The economy

- **Soldiers are no longer paid as though they sleep in the street.** Quarters
  and rations were missing entirely — a serving member paid full market rent
  out of a cash wage priced as if they did not. Service now carries the
  allowance it should, untaxed.
- **The runaway fortune is gone.** A scaled-up company could hold two hundred
  times what it took to open, where the design said twenty. That one number
  was most of how a business could quietly become worth more than the town.
- **The town buys its own houses.** Before, deeds only moved when somebody
  died or was foreclosed on, so the town's housing slowly drained into empty
  houses nobody could buy. Households now buy the home they live in when the
  bank will have them — and most of the time the bank will not, which is the
  honest shape of a small town.

## Smaller things

- Somebody who reaches a rung wanting papers goes and sits for them, instead
  of stopping there for the rest of their working life.
- A business that is about to fail warns you before the doors shut, and says
  what is wrong.
- Career paths that no town could have — a barista in 1970, a brand designer
  before 1980 — only appear once the world reaches them.
- The Jobs screen scrolls properly, and shows your traits and skills.
